You don't need a studio. You don't need expensive gear. You need your phone, decent lighting, and a system for creating content efficiently. This guide covers the fundamentals that separate amateur-looking content from professional UGC that brands want to pay for.

## Equipment: Start With What You Have

The best camera is the one in your pocket. Seriously — nearly all viral UGC is filmed on a smartphone. Brands actually *prefer* phone-shot content because it feels native to the platforms where it'll be posted.

### The essentials

**Your phone** — That's it. That's the most important piece of equipment. If it was made in the last 3–4 years, the camera is more than good enough.

**A clean lens** — This sounds obvious but it's the #1 reason videos look blurry or hazy. Wipe your camera lens with a soft cloth before every filming session. Your phone lives in your pocket, bag, and hands all day — the lens gets greasy.

<Callout variant="tip">
Before you film anything, wipe your lens. This single habit will instantly improve the quality of every video you make. Carry a small microfiber cloth or just use the corner of a clean t-shirt.
</Callout>

### Nice-to-have upgrades

Once you're consistently creating content and earning from it, consider these upgrades (in order of impact):

- **Ring light or desk lamp ($15–$30)** — Dramatically improves indoor video quality. Natural light is great but unpredictable. A simple ring light gives you consistent, flattering lighting any time of day.
- **Phone tripod ($10–$20)** — Stabilizes your shots and frees up your hands for demos. A basic one with a phone mount is all you need.
- **Wireless microphone ($25–$50)** — Important if you're doing voiceovers or talking directly to camera. Phone mics work but can pick up background noise.
- **Clip-on lens kit ($15–$25)** — Wide-angle lens is useful for showing products in context or filming in small spaces.

<Callout variant="warning">
Don't fall into the gear trap. New creators often use "I need better equipment" as an excuse not to start. You can create content that gets millions of views with a $200 phone and a window. Invest in gear only after you've proven you'll use it consistently.
</Callout>

## Lighting: The #1 Quality Factor

Lighting makes or breaks your video more than any other technical factor. Fortunately, great lighting is free.

### Natural light (free and best)

- **Face a window.** Position yourself so the window is in front of you (behind the camera). This gives you soft, even lighting on your face.
- **Avoid overhead light.** Ceiling lights create harsh shadows under your eyes and nose. Turn them off when you have good window light.
- **Shoot during "golden hours."** The best natural light is 1–2 hours after sunrise and 1–2 hours before sunset. But honestly, any daytime window light works great.
- **Overcast days are perfect.** Clouds act as a giant softbox, diffusing the light evenly.

### Indoor lighting tips

- **Two light sources > one.** If using artificial lights, place one in front of you and one slightly to the side. This eliminates harsh shadows.
- **Avoid mixed lighting.** Don't combine warm (yellowish) and cool (bluish) light sources — it makes your video look unnatural.
- **Ring lights are forgiving.** If you're unsure about lighting, a ring light placed directly behind your camera gives consistent, flattering results every time.

<Highlight emoji="💡" title="Quick lighting test">
Before filming, open your front-facing camera and check how you look. If one side of your face is noticeably darker than the other, adjust your position or add a light source. Your face should be evenly lit with no harsh shadows.
</Highlight>

## Filming Environment

Where you film matters. Your background tells a story — even if viewers don't consciously notice it.

### Indoor filming

- **Clean, uncluttered backgrounds.** A messy background is distracting. You don't need a fancy setup — just a tidy corner of your room.
- **Add visual interest.** A plant, a bookshelf, or even a simple colored wall is better than a blank white wall. Some texture and depth make your video more visually appealing.
- **Consistent "set."** Find one or two spots in your home that film well, and use them consistently. This saves setup time and creates a recognizable look for your content.

### Outdoor filming

- **Find shade.** Direct sunlight creates harsh shadows and makes you squint. Film under a tree, an awning, or on a porch.
- **Watch for wind.** Wind noise ruins audio. If it's windy, use a voiceover instead of live audio.
- **Be mindful of backgrounds.** No one wants to see a parking lot behind you (unless that's the vibe). Choose backgrounds that feel intentional.

## Batch Recording Workflow

This is the productivity hack that separates hobbyists from professionals. Instead of filming one video at a time, you batch record — filming multiple videos in a single session.

### Why batch recording works

- **Efficiency.** You only set up your filming environment once instead of every day.
- **Consistency.** Your lighting, outfit, and energy are consistent across videos.
- **Less decision fatigue.** Plan everything in advance, then just execute.
- **Buffer content.** Having 5–10 videos in drafts means you can post daily without filming daily.

### The batch recording process

<Step number={1} title="Plan your content (15 min)">
Decide what videos you're filming. Write out your hooks, key points, and CTAs for each video. Aim for 5–10 videos per batch session.
</Step>

<Step number={2} title="Set up your environment (10 min)">
Clean your background, set up lighting, clean your lens, charge your phone. Put it on Do Not Disturb mode.
</Step>

<Step number={3} title="Film everything (30–60 min)">
Run through your video list. Film each one 2–3 times so you have options in editing. Don't worry about perfection — you'll pick the best take later.
</Step>

<Step number={4} title="Save as drafts">
Upload each video to TikTok (or your primary platform) as a draft. Add captions, hashtags, and sounds later. Now you have a bank of content ready to post throughout the week.
</Step>

<Callout variant="tip">
Batch record in the same outfit? Film in a few different outfits during one session. Change your top between batches of 2–3 videos so it doesn't look like you filmed everything at once. A quick wardrobe swap takes 30 seconds.
</Callout>

## Posting Cadence

How often you post matters — not just for audience growth, but for how the algorithm treats your account.

### Recommended schedule

- **Minimum:** 1 video per day
- **Sweet spot:** 2–3 videos per day
- **Spacing:** Wait at least 2 hours between posts

<Callout variant="warning">
Posting multiple videos within an hour can trigger platform penalties. TikTok's algorithm may suppress your reach if it thinks you're spamming. Space your posts at least 2 hours apart — set reminders if you need to.
</Callout>

### When to post

The "best time to post" depends on your audience, but here are general guidelines:

- **Morning:** 7–9 AM (people checking phones before work/school)
- **Lunch:** 11 AM – 1 PM (break time scrolling)
- **Evening:** 7–10 PM (prime time — most engagement happens here)

Start with these windows and adjust based on your analytics after 2–3 weeks.

## Audio Strategy

Sound is half the experience on short-form platforms. Your audio choice directly impacts your reach.

### Trending sounds

- **Use trending sounds when possible.** The algorithm boosts content that uses popular audio.
- **How to find them:** Browse your For You Page and notice which sounds keep appearing. Use TikTok's "trending" filter in the sound library.
- **Match the vibe.** A trending sound only helps if it fits your content. Don't force a comedic sound on a serious product demo.

### Original audio

- **Use for voiceovers and tutorials.** When you're explaining something, original audio is often more effective than trending sounds.
- **Speak clearly and at a good pace.** Not too fast, not too slow. Practice your voiceover before recording.
- **Keep background noise minimal.** Film in a quiet space. Close windows, turn off fans, and put your phone on silent.

## Weekly Content Calendar

Here's a simple template for planning your week:

| Day | Content Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Product demo / review | Lead with your strongest content |
| Tuesday | Tutorial / how-to | Educational content builds trust |
| Wednesday | Trending format adaptation | Ride a current trend with your niche twist |
| Thursday | Before/after or transformation | High visual impact |
| Friday | Personal story / opinion | Build audience connection |
| Saturday | Batch film next week's content | Preparation day |
| Sunday | Engage + respond to comments | Community building |

<Callout variant="info">
This is a starting framework, not a rigid rule. Adapt it based on what performs well for your audience. After 2–3 weeks of posting, your analytics will tell you which content types get the most engagement.
</Callout>
