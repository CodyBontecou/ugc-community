The hook is the single most important part of your video. You have roughly 1.5–3 seconds to convince someone not to keep scrolling. A great hook creates a **curiosity gap** — the viewer *needs* to keep watching to fill that gap.

This library gives you 30+ proven hooks organized by category. Copy them, adapt them to your niche, and start using them immediately.

## What Makes a Great Hook

Before diving into the library, understand the three psychological triggers that make hooks work:

### 1. Curiosity gap

The viewer knows *something* is being promised but doesn't know *what* yet. They have to keep watching to find out.

> "I just found something that changed my entire routine..."

The viewer thinks: *"What did they find? I need to know."*

### 2. Emotional trigger

The hook connects to a feeling the viewer already has — frustration, desire, FOMO, excitement.

> "If you're tired of trying products that don't work..."

The viewer thinks: *"Yes! That's exactly how I feel."*

### 3. Pattern interrupt

The hook breaks expectations. Something visually or verbally surprising that makes the viewer pause.

> "I'm returning everything I bought this month... except this."

The viewer thinks: *"Wait, what? Why would they—oh, I need to see what they're keeping."*

<Callout variant="tip">
The best hooks combine two or three of these triggers. "Stop scrolling if you've been struggling with [problem]" uses urgency (pattern interrupt) + emotional trigger (connecting to their pain) + curiosity gap (what's the solution?).
</Callout>

## Discovery Hooks

Use these when you're introducing a product, tool, or method to your audience. They position you as someone who found something special.

1. **"I just found the [product type] that actually [delivers on a common promise]"**
   - *Example: "I just found the moisturizer that actually makes your skin glow overnight"*

2. **"Why is nobody talking about this [product type]?"**
   - *Example: "Why is nobody talking about this meal prep container?"*

3. **"I can't believe I just discovered [product/method]"**
   - *Example: "I can't believe I just discovered this way to organize my closet"*

4. **"The [product type] I didn't know I needed"**
   - *Example: "The kitchen gadget I didn't know I needed until now"*

5. **"If you haven't tried [product/method] yet, you need to see this"**
   - *Example: "If you haven't tried oil cleansing yet, you need to see this"*

6. **"I found the [product type] everyone's been looking for"**
   - *Example: "I found the protein bar that actually tastes good"*

## Transformation Hooks

Use these for before/after content, progress updates, or any video where you show a change.

7. **"Watch what happens when I use [product] for [time period]"**
   - *Example: "Watch what happens when I use this serum for 7 days"*

8. **"Before vs. after I started [method/product]"**
   - *Example: "Before vs. after I started using a standing desk"*

9. **"I tried [product] for [time period] — here are the results"**
   - *Example: "I tried cold plunging for 30 days — here are the results"*

10. **"My [routine/space/skin] 3 months ago vs. now"**
    - *Example: "My morning routine 3 months ago vs. now"*

11. **"One change that transformed my [area of life]"**
    - *Example: "One change that transformed my productivity"*

## Social Proof Hooks

These leverage the fact that other people are interested. FOMO is a powerful motivator.

12. **"Everyone keeps asking me about [product/method], so here it is"**
    - *Example: "Everyone keeps asking me about my skincare routine, so here it is"*

13. **"This [product] has [impressive number] reviews for a reason"**
    - *Example: "This blender has 50,000 5-star reviews for a reason"*

14. **"I finally tried the [product/trend] everyone's talking about"**
    - *Example: "I finally tried the viral hair mask everyone's talking about"*

15. **"POV: all your friends already know about this and you don't"**
    - *Example: Works as-is for any product reveal*

16. **"My most requested [product type] recommendation"**
    - *Example: "My most requested book recommendation"*

17. **"The [product] that made my friend ask 'what are you using?'"**
    - *Example: "The perfume that made my friend ask 'what are you wearing?'"*

## Urgency Hooks

Create a sense of immediacy — the viewer should feel like they need to act or pay attention *right now*.

18. **"Stop scrolling if you [relatable problem/desire]"**
    - *Example: "Stop scrolling if you struggle with falling asleep at night"*

19. **"You need to see this before [event/season/deadline]"**
    - *Example: "You need to see this before summer starts"*

20. **"Drop everything — I just found [amazing thing]"**
    - *Example: "Drop everything — I just found the best dry shampoo that ever existed"*

21. **"If you see this, it's a sign to [action]"**
    - *Example: "If you see this, it's a sign to finally start your skincare routine"*

22. **"I almost didn't post this but [reason]"**
    - *Example: "I almost didn't post this but I can't keep this product a secret anymore"*

## Contrarian Hooks

Challenge common beliefs or go against the grain. These create engagement because people either strongly agree or want to argue.

23. **"Nobody talks about [underrated thing] and it's a problem"**
    - *Example: "Nobody talks about the importance of scalp care and it's a problem"*

24. **"Unpopular opinion: [bold statement about your niche]"**
    - *Example: "Unpopular opinion: expensive skincare isn't better than drugstore"*

25. **"Everything you've been told about [topic] is wrong"**
    - *Example: "Everything you've been told about washing your face is wrong"*

26. **"I'm done pretending [popular product/method] works"**
    - *Example: "I'm done pretending expensive gym memberships are worth it"*

## Relatability Hooks

Connect with viewers through shared experiences. These build community and trust.

27. **"POV: you just [relatable moment]"**
    - *Example: "POV: you just found out your skincare routine has been making things worse"*

28. **"Me when I [relatable situation] and discover [product]"**
    - *Example: "Me when I'm stress-eating at 2am and discover healthy snack alternatives"*

29. **"Tell me you [identity/trait] without telling me you [identity/trait]"**
    - *Example: "Tell me you're a skincare addict without telling me you're a skincare addict"*

30. **"Things I wish I knew before I started [activity]"**
    - *Example: "Things I wish I knew before I started working out at home"*

## Product Demo Hooks

Specifically designed for when you're showcasing a product — the bread and butter of UGC work.

31. **"Let me show you why I use [product] every single day"**
    - *Example: "Let me show you why I use this planner every single day"*

32. **"I've tried every [product type] and this is the only one that [benefit]"**
    - *Example: "I've tried every mascara and this is the only one that doesn't smudge"*

33. **"The [product] that replaced [old product/method] in my routine"**
    - *Example: "The app that replaced my physical planner, notes, and calendar"*

34. **"Honest review: is [product] worth the hype?"**
    - *Example: "Honest review: is the viral Stanley Cup worth the hype?"*

35. **"3 things about [product] that surprised me"**
    - *Example: "3 things about this air fryer that surprised me"*

## How to Adapt Hooks for Your Niche

Every hook in this library works as a template. Here's how to customize them:

<Step number={1} title="Identify the structure">
Look at the hook template and find the variables — the parts in [brackets]. These are what you'll replace with your specific content.
</Step>

<Step number={2} title="Fill in your niche">
Replace the variables with products, problems, and benefits specific to your niche. "I just found the [product type] that actually [benefit]" becomes "I just found the moisturizer that actually doesn't clog pores."
</Step>

<Step number={3} title="Test the curiosity gap">
Read your finished hook out loud. Does it make you want to know more? Would you stop scrolling? If not, try a different template or tweak the wording.
</Step>

## Writing Your Own Hooks

Once you've used these templates enough, you'll start writing original hooks. Here's the framework:

**Start with the viewer's desire or pain point.** What does your target audience want? What frustrates them?

**Create a gap.** Promise something without fully revealing it. "I found something..." is better than "I found this specific moisturizer brand."

**Add urgency or emotion.** "Stop scrolling," "You need to see this," "I can't believe" — these phrases create immediate engagement.

**Keep it under 10 words.** Short hooks are more powerful. If your hook needs 20 words, it's not a hook — it's a paragraph.

<Callout variant="info">
Keep a "hook swipe file." Every time you see a hook that makes you stop scrolling, screenshot it. Over time, you'll build a personal library of proven hooks you can reference whenever you need inspiration.
</Callout>
